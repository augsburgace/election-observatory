# Early-vote interpretation and forecast audit

The `ev-geography-research-1.3` model corrects two interpretation problems in earlier conditional research calls. It does not establish a validated nationwide EV forecasting system.

## Corrections

- Historical Senate references use the same election environment: midterm, presidential or off-year. Different-type outcomes remain context with zero weight. A January runoff retains its underlying election cycle.
- Final-election geographic composition cannot move the live EV point. An adjustment requires an earlier same-type, same-stage EV distribution, matching method/status definitions, synchronous current observations, and sufficient matched participation in both current and historical geography.
- Native definitions are compared explicitly. Two reviewed NCSBE cast-ballot descriptions share a narrow semantic alias. Cast, received, accepted and issued measures are not equated elsewhere. County/state definitions must agree before numeric reconciliation.
- Historical training uses only earlier outcomes of the target election type. Repeated snapshots and related contests share state/election weights; later runoff labels cannot enter earlier training.
- A one-method turnout extrapolation cannot label people who already used another method as still waiting to vote.
- The national forecast map defaults to eligible EV comparisons. Historical-only colored references require a separate view.
- Every model run checks reference types, normalized weights, adjustment eligibility and point arithmetic before caching/publication. Original observations and historical revisions are preserved.

## Findings at revision 592, cutoff October 21, 2026

The old Ohio R+2.8 point combined a roughly R+2.2 mixed-cycle Senate reference with a +0.59-point adjustment against final-election geography. That did not establish a same-stage EV advantage. The corrected Ohio point is approximately R+1.1 **historical context**, using the 2018 and 2022 Senate outcomes, with no EV adjustment. The 763,784 reported early ballots remain participation evidence. Different candidates, seats and current conditions make this historical number a weak standalone current-election forecast.

The old final-geography audit had 26 dependent checkpoints covering nine state/election outcomes, with about 9.7 points mean absolute margin error. It cannot establish accurate nationwide calls. The corrected headline evaluates the same-stage feature eligible for live adjustment: seven checkpoints covering three state/election outcomes across two election years. On those same cases, the same-type historical reference has about 5.0 points MAE and the frozen EV assumption about 4.4 points MAE. This is too small to establish generalization, superiority over polling, calibrated intervals, or winner probabilities. The samples differ from the old final-geography sample; their headline errors are not a before/after performance comparison.

The audit panel reports the matching state, election type, method/status and stage-feature subset. Zero means no matching validation, even when broader exploratory cases exist. North Carolina has compatible current same-stage geographic evidence; that alone does not validate the assumed conversion to a candidate margin.

## Poll and market disagreements

Outside-source switches remain off by default. Enabling them adds separate survey-margin bars and market-price cards. A five-point survey gap, opposite survey direction, or contrary majority-priced party contract triggers inspection. Five points is an editorial review threshold, not a statistical significance test. Each study remains separate; candidate configurations, shared samples, field dates and populations require inspection. Payout prices are never converted into vote margins. The research point never moves just to match an outside source.

## Methodological sources

- [Census, 2022 congressional-election report](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024/congressional-election-voting-report.html): turnout and early/mail use changed even between midterms. Match election type, then inspect methods and calendars.
- [EAC, 2024 administrative findings](https://www.eac.gov/sites/default/files/2025-06/EAVS_2024_One_Sheet_06_30_25_508.pdf): early in-person and Election Day are separate methods; growth in one is not automatically growth in final turnout.
- [Pew, validated-voter method research](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2025/08/22/majority-of-americans-continue-to-back-expanded-early-voting-voting-by-mail-voter-id/): party-method patterns change. Its 2024 research reports Election Day voting among 38% of Trump voters and 29% of Harris voters. This methodological reference supplies no polling adjustment or inferred votes to the EV model.
- [EAC, Ohio affiliation definition](https://www.eac.gov/ohio-voter-info): affiliation follows partisan-primary participation rather than registration declaration; it is not general-election vote choice.
- [EAC, Texas affiliation definition](https://www.eac.gov/taxonomy/term/18371): Texas has no party registration. Primary-history groups must retain their classification window. A large early-vote fraction does not identify the winner.

Remaining limits include incomplete same-stage archives, unverified current candidate/seat correspondence, changing rules/geography, incomplete denominators and limited independent historical outcomes. Religion, race, propensity and candidate support are not manufactured from county population composition. These distinctions must remain visible as coverage expands.
